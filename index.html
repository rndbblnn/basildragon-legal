Summary (plain language)

Basil Dragon helps you track your groceries and reduce waste. To do that it reads photos of your receipts and your fridge/pantry using AI, and keeps an approximate list of what you have. We designed it to hold as little as possible:

  • Your photos are processed, not stored. Receipt and pantry photos are sent to our AI providers to read them; we do not store the images.
  • We do not sell your data and there is no advertising.
  • Your data lives on servers in the United States.
  • You can export or delete everything — in a shared kitchen, your own data — at any time, from inside the app.

We do not collect: precise location, contacts, advertising IDs, or device fingerprints. (We do work out an approximate location from your IP address — see “Your approximate location” below.) (The flyer regions you pick are store areas you choose, not your location, and are kept apart from it.) There is no account: no email, no phone number, no address. We do record usage events of our own — which screen you reached, whether a scan succeeded and how long it took, whether a reply was useful — so we can tell what works and what breaks. These are counts and labels, never the contents of your kitchen: no product names, no prices, no receipt lines, nothing you typed. Your approximate location, from your IP address. Every time the app talks to our servers, your phone’s IP address reaches our hosting provider (Railway, United States), which keeps it in its request logs together with the address of the request — and that address contains your kitchen’s identifier. When we look up a kitchen (for example because you wrote to us, or to check whether a kitchen belongs to a real person or to an automated test device), we can match those logs to the kitchen and work out from the IP address roughly where the connection came from — a city or region and country — and which internet provider or network it belongs to. We do this with a geolocation database kept on our own computer: your IP address is not sent to any outside location service. We never use GPS or your phone’s location permission (the app does not ask for it), and this is never precise enough to find a street address. We use it only for support and to understand how the service is used — never for advertising, and it is never sold.

  • How long: the request logs are kept by our hosting provider. In our experience they reach back about one month, but we have not yet confirmed the exact retention period with the provider. The approximate location is not saved in our database or in anything we publish. It can appear in our internal support notes — our working sessions, in which an AI assistant from Anthropic (United States) helps us as our service provider — and we have not yet set a retention period for those notes.
  • Choice: it is part of how the service works, so it cannot be turned off inside the app. Deleting your account removes your kitchen from our database, but does not erase our hosting provider’s existing request logs before they expire. The data export (Settings → Export everything) covers our database and does not include these logs; to ask what they hold about you, email us (see Contact).

Flyer deals on your shopping list (since 2026-10-12). If you pick one or more flyer regions (up to 3), our servers compare your shopping list with the public flyer deals published for those regions and show a deal line under a matching item, for example “Flyer at IGA · Laval: Lactantia 2 L, 4,99 $, until Wed”. Flyers are public information we collect ourselves; a store never receives anything about you, nobody pays us to show a deal, and no deal is a recommendation.

  • Which items: only items a person in your kitchen put on the list. Items that landed there because Basil Dragon guessed they were running out, or that a connected assistant added, are not matched.
  • How: the match is done by our own code, not by an AI model, and your list never leaves our servers for it. (Separately, we use Google’s AI to tie printed flyer lines to our product catalog; that step uses only the public flyer text and never sees any kitchen.)
  • What is stored: your chosen regions (see the table above). The deals themselves are public flyer facts, stored apart from the prices on your receipts, and never used for your usual price. After you confirm a receipt, our own code (no AI) compares its lines with flyer deals in your chosen regions to show when you paid the flyer price; we keep nothing from that comparison on our servers (the result stays in your chat, on your phone). The app records that a deal line was shown, tapped or hidden, without the product or the price.
  • Not the same item: if you hide a deal, we remember that for your kitchen (and which member did it) so it stays hidden for everyone in your kitchen and for connected assistants. It is in your export and deleted with your account.
  • Choice: it is off until you pick a region, and clearing your regions turns it off.

Price estimates (since 2026-10-12). When you ask Basil Dragon to plan your week, the plan comes with an estimated cost. For each item to buy, our own code uses, in order: the printed price from this week’s flyers in the flyer regions you chose; the prices on your own confirmed receipts from the last 180 days; and, when neither exists, a typical price pooled from the confirmed receipts of at least 5 other kitchens.

  • What goes to Google: your chat message (as with any assistant chat turn) and this week’s printed flyer lines for your chosen regions: the store, the product and the printed price text. These lines are public flyer facts, but because they come from the regions you chose, your region may be inferable from them. The amounts from your receipts and the pooled prices are not sent to Google; our own code adds up the total.
  • Pooled prices are statistics. A pooled price is a typical (median) price worked out from at least 5 other kitchens' receipts. In a week plan it only ever appears inside a rounded total. Estimates for individual items may also use pooled prices; pooled prices never identify another kitchen, its stores, or when it shopped.
  • Your receipts count too: the prices on the receipts you confirm contribute to the pooled prices other kitchens see, in the same way. Receipt lines that our AI matched to an item on your shopping list are kept for your own price history but left out of pooled prices. When you delete your account, your price records are deleted and the pooled prices are recalculated without them (see “Price records” below).

AI processing (third parties)

To read photos and text, we send them to:

  • Google (Gemini) — reads receipts and photos, extracts text/items; and, since 2026-07-19, answers the messages you type in the assistant chat and, so its suggestions fit you, your saved kitchen profile (dietary preferences, allergies, and household size). Since 2026-08-19 the same kitchen profile is also sent when you tap for shopping-list suggestions, if you agreed to that use (see below), together with what’s in your kitchen and the names of products you’ve bought before — including ones you no longer have — so the list it suggests fits your household rather than being generic. That request sends names only: not what you paid, not where you shopped, not when. You have to ask for it; nothing is sent unless you tap. The suggestions themselves are not saved on our servers — your phone keeps the last answer so the list is there when you come back, and a suggestion becomes part of your data only if you tap it onto your list. Chat messages are sent to Google to generate a reply and are not stored on our servers (no chat transcript is saved server-side; the one thing a chat can save is a week plan you ask for — see “Your kitchen’s saved week plan” above). Since 2026-08-11 your phone keeps the recent conversation — about the last 50 messages — in the app’s own storage, so it is still there when you come back. We keep no copy on our servers. Deleting your account in Settings erases it from the phone as well. If you use Android’s own backup, your phone’s backup may include it, like the rest of the app’s data. Since 2026-08-06 there is one deliberate exception, and only with your tap: if you tell the assistant an allergy, a diet or how many people you cook for, it can offer to remember it, and confirming saves that value (not the message) to your kitchen profile, where you can edit or delete it in Settings; the kitchen profile is stored in your account and — since 2026-07-23 — sent to Google with every assistant chat turn (previously only when suggesting a recipe). Since 2026-07-20, product names (from your scans or manual adds) are also sent to Google once per catalog product, to match, name and illustrate the shared item catalog: to generate the small generic item picture shown in lists, to recognise when a newly seen product is one we already have, and to tidy up the stored product name (capitalisation, and the French or English version when only one was captured). The picture and the tidied name are shared by every user, contain no personal information (no people, text, or logos), and the result (not your name transmission) is what we store. Because a corrected name replaces the wording an item was first saved under, the name shown in your kitchen may change to a cleaner version of the same product; the product itself is never changed. Since 2026-10-12, when you ask the assistant to plan your week, this week’s printed flyer lines for your chosen flyer regions are sent along with your message (see “Price estimates” above).
  • Anthropic (Claude) — used for harder cases.

Connected AI assistants (optional, since 2026-09-25)

You can connect an AI assistant of your choosing — for example Claude, Meta Muse, or any assistant that supports the Model Context Protocol (MCP) — to your kitchen, by entering a pairing code shown in the app. Nothing is shared until you do this. In a kitchen shared with other members, only the kitchen’s owner can connect an assistant, and the connection shares the whole kitchen — including items other members added.

  • What the assistant can see: the items in your kitchen (name, category, in / low / out, days left, brand/variant label, size, where it’s kept, expiry date) and your shopping list. An assistant connected from app 0.1.34 or later also sees, and can add, the short shopping notes you write on list items (for example “x2, the big tub”); a note is deleted as soon as the item is bought or removed, and we keep no history of it. Since 2026-09-27 it also receives how each item was first added (receipt, photo, by hand, or by an assistant) — never when — and, for items in the freezer, a general “typically keeps for” guide for that kind of food, which is a rough estimate, not a date.
  • Flyer deals (since 2026-10-12): an assistant approved in app 0.1.34 or later can also see the flyer regions your kitchen picked and the public flyer deals in them — by default only deals matching items a person put on your shopping list, and on request other deals from those regions (store, region, product, brand, size, printed price, any condition, and the last valid day). It cannot change your regions, and it is not shown deals your kitchen hid with “Not the same item”.
  • What it can do: manage your kitchen and shopping list — add items to your list, add items to your kitchen with details like size, where it’s kept and expiry, and restock items you have (which takes them off your list). It can add a product Basil Dragon doesn’t know yet: that product is then treated exactly like one found in a photo scan — our AI providers may tidy its name and draw its picture, and it becomes available to other kitchens' product search like every other catalog product. A connection can also use new features Basil Dragon adds later, without asking you again; connections made before 2026-09-26 received these abilities automatically. The exceptions are marking items and reading your kitchen profile (below): each is available only to a connection approved with a consent text that described it.
  • Marking and checking off (since 2026-09-27): an assistant can also mark an item in your kitchen as used up or running low — only for connections approved with the full-access consent text (app 0.1.32 and later) — and check items off your shopping list, which marks them bought and restocks them in your kitchen.
  • Your shared cart (since 2026-10-01): the same connections (full-access consent text, app 0.1.32 and later) can also put an item from your shopping list in your kitchen’s shared cart, or take it out. Every member of your kitchen sees that tick. It is not a purchase, and the assistant is never told who ticked an item or when.
  • Your kitchen profile (since app 0.1.34, 2026-09-27): a connection approved in app 0.1.34 or later — whose consent text says so — can also read the kitchen’s saved dietary preferences, allergies and household size, and use them to suggest recipes from what you have. It can read them only while AI processing is turned on for the kitchen (in a shared kitchen, only while every member has agreed to it); when it is off, the assistant receives nothing from the profile and is told to ask you instead. Connections approved in earlier versions of the app cannot read the profile. Allergies are health information and a diet can reveal religious practice: once the assistant’s company receives them, its own privacy policy governs them, and its suggestions are not a medical safety system — keep reading labels.
  • What it never receives: your receipts themselves (images, line items, store, prices or dates), prices, photos or chat history.
  • Who receives it: the company that runs the assistant you connected — you choose it, and its own privacy policy governs what it does with the data. That company is not our service provider and may process the data outside Québec and Canada (typically the United States). Your kitchen data goes from our servers directly to that assistant; it does not pass through anyone else.
  • Sign-in (WorkOS): we use WorkOS (United States) only to run the connection’s sign-in step. WorkOS receives a random connection identifier and a made-up, undeliverable address (<identifier>@connect.basildragon.invalid) — never your name, your email or any kitchen data.
  • Seeing and disconnecting: every member of the kitchen can see its connections in Settings → Connected assistants, and any member can disconnect any of them at any time. A connection stays active when someone joins the kitchen; it is disconnected when the seat of the person who connected it is removed or when they delete their account. Data the assistant already received stays with its company.
  • Export: your connections (when each was made, which consent text was agreed to, when it was last used and when it was disconnected) are included in the data export (Settings → Export everything). Since 2026-09-27 the export also includes the kitchen actions recorded as done by you or by an assistant you connected, each labelled performed_by (you or your_assistant); the record of who did each action is cleared when your seat is erased.

Assistants IA connectés (facultatif, depuis le 2026-09-25). Vous pouvez connecter l'assistant IA de votre choix — par exemple Claude, Meta Muse ou tout assistant compatible avec le Model Context Protocol (MCP) — à votre cuisine, en entrant un code de jumelage affiché dans l'appli. Rien n'est partagé avant ça. Dans une cuisine partagée avec d'autres membres, seul le propriétaire de la cuisine peut connecter un assistant, et la connexion partage toute la cuisine — y compris les articles ajoutés par les autres membres.

  • Ce que l'assistant peut voir : les articles de votre cuisine (nom, catégorie, en stock / presque fini / probablement fini, jours restants, marque ou variante, format, rangement, date d'expiration) et votre liste d'épicerie. Un assistant connecté depuis l'appli 0.1.34 ou plus récente voit aussi, et peut ajouter, les courtes notes d'épicerie que vous écrivez sur les articles de votre liste (par exemple « x2, le gros pot »); une note est supprimée dès que l'article est acheté ou retiré, et nous n'en gardons aucun historique.
  • Comment chaque article a été ajouté (depuis le 2026-09-27) : il reçoit aussi la manière dont chaque article a été ajouté à l'origine (facture, photo, à la main ou par un assistant) — jamais quand — ainsi que, pour les articles au congélateur, une indication générale du temps de conservation habituel pour ce type d'aliment — une estimation approximative, pas une date.
  • Rabais des circulaires (depuis le 2026-10-12) : un assistant approuvé dans l'appli 0.1.34 ou plus récente voit aussi les régions de circulaires choisies pour votre cuisine et les rabais publics annoncés dans ces circulaires — par défaut, seulement celles qui correspondent à des articles qu'une personne a mis sur votre liste d'épicerie et, sur demande, les autres rabais de ces régions (magasin, région, produit, marque, format, prix imprimé, condition s'il y a lieu et dernier jour de validité). Il ne peut pas modifier vos régions, et il ne voit pas les rabais que votre cuisine a masqués avec « Ce n’est pas le même article ».

  • Ce qu'il peut faire : gérer votre cuisine et votre liste d'épicerie — ajouter des articles à votre liste, ajouter des articles à votre cuisine avec des détails comme le format, le rangement et l'expiration, et réapprovisionner des articles que vous avez (le réapprovisionnement les retire de votre liste). Il peut ajouter un produit inconnu de Basil Dragon : ce produit est alors traité exactement comme un produit trouvé par une photo — nos fournisseurs d'IA peuvent en nettoyer le nom et en dessiner l'image, et il devient accessible dans la recherche de produits des autres cuisines, comme tout autre produit du catalogue. Une connexion peut aussi utiliser les nouvelles fonctionnalités que Basil Dragon ajoutera plus tard, sans vous redemander; les connexions faites avant le 2026-09-26 ont reçu ces capacités automatiquement. Deux exceptions : marquer des articles et lire votre profil de cuisine (ci-dessous) ne sont offerts qu'à une connexion approuvée avec un texte de consentement qui les décrivait.

  • Marquer et cocher (depuis le 2026-09-27) : un assistant peut aussi marquer un article de votre cuisine comme probablement fini ou presque fini — seulement pour les connexions approuvées avec le texte de consentement à accès complet (appli 0.1.32 et plus récente) — et cocher des articles de votre liste d'épicerie, ce qui les marque comme achetés et les réapprovisionne dans votre cuisine.

  • Votre panier partagé (depuis le 2026-10-01) : ces mêmes connexions (texte de consentement à accès complet, appli 0.1.32 et plus récente) peuvent aussi mettre un article de votre liste d'épicerie dans le panier partagé de votre cuisine, ou l'en retirer. Tous les membres de votre cuisine voient cette coche. Ce n'est pas un achat, et l'assistant n'est jamais informé de qui a coché un article, ni quand.

  • Votre profil de cuisine (depuis l'appli 0.1.34, le 2026-09-27) : une connexion approuvée dans l'appli 0.1.34 ou plus récente — dont le texte de consentement le mentionne — peut aussi lire les préférences alimentaires, les allergies et le nombre de personnes pour qui vous cuisinez enregistrés pour la cuisine, et s'en servir pour suggérer des recettes avec ce que vous avez. Elle ne peut les lire que lorsque le traitement par IA est activé pour la cuisine (dans une cuisine partagée, seulement lorsque tous les membres y ont consenti); s'il est désactivé, l'assistant ne reçoit rien du profil et on lui dit de vous poser la question. Les connexions approuvées dans une version antérieure de l'appli ne peuvent pas lire le profil. Les allergies sont des renseignements sur la santé et un régime peut révéler une pratique religieuse : une fois reçus par l'entreprise de l'assistant, c'est sa propre politique de confidentialité qui s'applique, et ses suggestions ne sont pas un système de sécurité médicale — continuez de lire les étiquettes.

  • Ce qu'il ne reçoit jamais : vos factures elles-mêmes (images, lignes d'articles, magasin, prix ou dates), vos prix, vos photos ou votre historique de clavardage.

  • Qui reçoit les données : vos données de cuisine vont directement de nos serveurs à l'entreprise qui exploite l'assistant que vous avez choisi — elles ne transitent par personne d'autre. Cette entreprise n'est pas notre fournisseur de services ; sa propre politique de confidentialité s'applique, et elle peut traiter les données à l'extérieur du Québec et du Canada (généralement aux États-Unis).
  • Connexion (WorkOS) : nous utilisons WorkOS (États-Unis) uniquement pour l'étape de connexion : WorkOS reçoit un identifiant aléatoire et une adresse fictive impossible à livrer — jamais votre nom, votre courriel ni les données de votre cuisine.
  • Voir et déconnecter : tous les membres de la cuisine voient ses connexions dans Réglages → Assistants connectés, et n'importe quel membre peut en déconnecter une en tout temps. Une connexion reste active quand quelqu'un se joint à la cuisine ; elle est retirée si la place de la personne qui l'a créée est supprimée ou si cette personne supprime son compte. Subscriptions and payments

Basil Dragon offers one optional paid plan, Basil Dragon Plus, billed by Google Play monthly or yearly (a subscription bought on the earlier 3-month plan keeps renewing every three months). To manage the subscription (grant access after you buy, keep it active on renewal, remove access if you cancel), we use RevenueCat (United States), a subscription-management service. RevenueCat receives your household’s identifier (the same pseudonymous identifier described above — not your name or email), which product you purchased, and the subscription’s status from Google Play; it does not receive your card details. RevenueCat acts only on our behalf, under contract, to run the subscription — it does not use this information for its own advertising or resale.

The free plan requires no purchase. Every copy of the app, on the free plan too, connects to RevenueCat when it starts and gives it your household’s pseudonymous identifier, so that a purchase can be matched to your kitchen if you ever make one; RevenueCat’s software also sends basic technical details such as the app version and the kind of device. Without a purchase, RevenueCat holds no subscription or payment information about you. Each calendar month, a free household can use up to 4 receipt scans, 10 photos and 15 assistant chat messages; when one runs out, that feature pauses until the next month begins. Every free household also gets a modest, one-time allowance of AI use that does not renew; once it’s used, AI features (scanning, photos, the assistant) pause until you subscribe. Either way, your saved kitchen, lists and past corrections, and adding items by hand, keep working (within the item limit), and nothing is ever deleted for not paying. A kitchen on the free plan can keep up to 75 items, and one on Basil Dragon Plus up to 500. Each separate entry counts as one item — a product together with its brand or label, so two brands of milk are two items; items in the kitchen and on the shopping list count together, and so do items a connected assistant adds. At the limit, only adding a new item is refused. If Basil Dragon Plus ends while a kitchen has more than 75 items, nothing is deleted and every existing item keeps working, but no new item can be added until the kitchen has fewer than 75 items or Basil Dragon Plus resumes.

About the allergy filter, plainly. We do two things with the allergies you save: we tell the AI to avoid them, and — for the shopping suggestions — we also check the suggested product names ourselves and drop any that name an allergen you listed, in English or French. That second check is a real safeguard and it catches mistakes the AI makes. But it works on names, so it cannot see an allergen that is simply an ingredient of something: a dish or a prepared product whose name doesn’t mention it can get through. Treat it as a best-effort filter, not a medical safety system — the same wording as our Terms — and keep reading labels. If you tell us about an allergy and something still slips past, that is a limitation we are naming here rather than a promise we broke.
